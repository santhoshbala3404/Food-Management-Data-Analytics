# Food Management Data Analytics

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing food management and distribution data to understand food availability, provider contributions, receiver requirements, and food claim patterns.

Using SQL and Snowflake, the project explores relationships between food providers, receivers, food listings, and claims. A Streamlit application is also developed to display SQL query results through an interactive dashboard.

The objective is to understand food distribution patterns and identify opportunities to improve food utilization and reduce food wastage.

## Tools & Technologies
- **SQL:** Data querying, joins, aggregations, and analysis.
- **Snowflake:** Cloud data warehouse.
- **Python:** Application development and data handling.
- **Pandas:** Data manipulation.
- **Streamlit:** Interactive dashboard development.

## Key Areas of Analysis

### 1. Provider and Receiver Analysis
- Number of food providers across cities.
- Number of receivers across cities.
- Provider contribution patterns.
- Provider information and distribution locations.

### 2. Food Availability Analysis
- Total quantity of available food.
- Most frequently listed food items.
- Food availability across different locations.
- Distribution of food by meal type and food category.

### 3. Claims and Distribution Analysis
- Food claim counts.
- Completed and pending claim status distribution.
- Successful food distribution.
- Provider contribution and receiver claim patterns.

## Key Insights
- Total available food quantity is 25,794 units.
- Rice, Soup, and Salad are among the most frequently listed food items.
- Breakfast is the most common meal type.
- Vegetarian, Vegan, and Non-Vegetarian food categories have relatively balanced representation.
- New Carol and South Kathryn have the highest provider counts, with six providers each.
- Supermarkets are the most common provider type.

## Project Files
- `food_management_sql_project.sql` – SQL queries for food management analysis.
- `food_management_streamlit_project.ipynb` – Streamlit dashboard implementation.
- `Food Storage Insights Report.pdf` – Analysis findings and insights.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how SQL, Snowflake, and Streamlit can be used to analyze food availability and distribution. The insights can help improve coordination between providers and receivers and support better utilization of available food resources.
